2.1 Basic Motion Control ​

2.1.1 Gait Overview ​

  • Gait Concept

Robot gait describes the periodic locomotion phenomenon observed in animals and refers to the coordinated movement sequence and trajectory of each robot leg. It serves as a vital factor in ensuring the stable operation of a legged mechanism. Research into gaits primarily aims to achieve stable periodic locomotion for legged robots.

In everyday terms, gait describes how animals walk. Common gait patterns observed in hexapod insects include the tripod gait and ripple gait.

Tripod Gait: Three legs alternate with the other three legs to support the body. Specifically, when the left front leg touches the ground, the right rear leg and left rear leg contact the ground simultaneously, followed by the right front leg. This gait ensures stability during movement and minimizes energy consumption.

Ripple Gait: All six legs contact the ground in a phased rhythmic sequence. Specifically, when the left front leg contacts the ground, the other five legs follow in sequence with specified phase delays. This gait enhances movement speed, smoothness, and body coordination across varied terrain.

The table below lists common terminology used in gait analysis:

TermDescription
PhaseRepresents the angular position or relative state within a cyclic motion.
Phase differenceDescribes the advance or lag in motion between different legs.
Swing phaseRefers to the state where a leg is lifted and suspended in the air.
Stance phaseRefers to the state where a leg contacts the ground and supports the body.
CycleDescribes the complete motion sequence from the touch-down of one foot to its subsequent touch-down during locomotion.
Gait frequencyIndicates the number of gait cycles completed per unit of time.
Step lengthRepresents the distance traveled by the foot tip from lifting off to touching down within a single cycle.
Stride lengthRepresents the total displacement of the robot chassis within a single cycle.
Duty cycleIndicates the ratio of the duration a single leg spends in the stance phase to the total gait cycle duration.

2.2 Tripod Gait ​

2.2.1 Tripod Gait Overview ​

The tripod gait is a classic locomotion method for hexapod walking robots. When hexapod insects walk, all six legs do not move forward in a straight line simultaneously. Instead, the three pairs of legs are divided into two alternating triangular support structures that advance in sequence. In simple terms, two groups of three legs alternate lifting and lowering.

Under this triangular support structure, the chassis maintains static stability at all times.

In tripod gait, each leg swings according to a specific sequence. Typically, three legs swing forward during a step while the other three legs push backward in contact with the ground. This gait helps the robot maintain equilibrium and traverse uneven terrain effectively.

Key advantages of the tripod gait for hexapod robots include:

  1. High stability: Because three legs always support the body while forming a stable support polygon, the robot resists tipping over and maintains equilibrium. This enables the hexapod robot to perform reliably on uneven terrain.

  2. Strong adaptability: Tripod gait allows the robot to adapt flexibly to varying terrain and environmental conditions. Because the foot trajectories form triangular support bases, the robot can negotiate obstacles and climb slopes with ease.

  3. Exceptional agility: The tripod gait enables rapid direction and speed changes. This allows the robot to respond quickly in dynamic scenarios, such as obstacle avoidance and target tracking in confined spaces.

The diagrams below illustrate the four phases of the tripod gait cycle:

As shown in Phase A, legs 1, 3, and 5 lift and swing forward, while legs 2, 4, and 6 remain on the ground to support the chassis, ensuring that the center of gravity of the robot stays within the diagonal support polygon. At this moment, legs 1, 3, and 5 are in the swing phase, and legs 2, 4, and 6 are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase B, all six legs contact the ground simultaneously. Legs 2, 4, and 6 maintain their positions, while legs 1, 3, and 5 land in forward positions. All six legs are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase C, legs 2, 4, and 6 lift and swing forward, while legs 1, 3, and 5 support the chassis, ensuring that the center of gravity remains within the diagonal support polygon. At this moment, legs 2, 4, and 6 are in the swing phase, and legs 1, 3, and 5 are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase D, all six legs contact the ground simultaneously. Legs 1, 3, and 5 maintain their positions, while legs 2, 4, and 6 land in forward positions. All six legs are in the stance phase.

Completing the four phases, Phase A through Phase D, constitutes one full motion cycle for JetHexa.

class TripodGait(Node):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__('tripod_gait')
        self.controller = ControllerClient()
        self.client = self.create_client(Trigger, '/controller_manager/init_finish')
        self.client.wait_for_service()

    def move(
        self,
        gait=1,              # Gait type: 0 for stop, 1 for ripple gait, 2 for tripod gait
        stride=40.0,         # Stride length (mm), default is 40 mm, range: 0–65
        height=15.0,         # Step height (mm), default is 15 mm, range: 0–50
        direction=0,         # Movement direction (degrees), range: 0°–360°, 0° for forward, increasing counterclockwise
        rotation=0.0,        # Rotation rate, default is no rotation; positive for counterclockwise, negative for clockwise
        time=1,              # Single step duration (seconds), default is 1 s
        steps=0,             # Number of steps, 0 indicates continuous motion
        interrupt=True,      # Whether interruption by new commands is allowed, default is True
        relative_height=False # Whether height is relative to the ground, default is absolute height
    ):
        """Method to control robot movement

        Args:
            gait (int): Gait type (e.g., 1 = ripple gait)
            stride (float): Stride length (mm)
            height (float): Leg lifting height (mm)
            direction (float): Movement direction (0 = forward, 180 = backward)
            rotation (float): Rotation angle (positive for turn right, negative for turn left)
            time (float): Execution time for a single step (seconds)
            steps (int): Number of movement steps (0 = continuous)
            interrupt (bool): Whether to allow interruption by new commands
            relative_height (bool): Whether height is relative
        """
        self.controller.traveling(
            gait=gait,
            stride=stride,
            height=height,
            direction=direction,
            rotation=rotation,
            time=time,
            steps=steps,
            interrupt=interrupt,
            relative_height=relative_height
        )

The program controls robot motion primarily by configuring the parameters of the move function:

  • gait: Specifies the gait mode. A value of 0 stops motion, 1 selects ripple gait, and 2 selects tripod gait.
  • stride: Sets the stride length in millimeters. The default range is 0–65 mm. Values outside this range may cause the robot to stall or stumble.
  • height: Sets the step height in millimeters. The default range is 0–50 mm. Values outside this range may cause the robot to stall or stumble.
  • direction: Sets the movement direction in degrees, with a range of 0°–360°. A value between 0° and 180° moves the robot forward, while a value between 180° and 360° moves the robot backward.
  • rotation: Sets the turning rate in radians per second. A positive value rotates the robot counterclockwise, and a negative value rotates the robot clockwise.
  • time: Sets the time interval for each individual step in seconds.
  • steps: Sets the number of movement steps. A value of 0 indicates continuous movement.
  • interrupt: Sets whether new commands can interrupt the ongoing execution.
  • relative_height: Specifies whether the step height parameter height is relative to the ground. A value of False uses relative height.

2.3 Ripple Gait ​

2.3.1 Ripple Gait Overview ​

The ripple gait, also known as wave gait, provides smooth continuous locomotion. The term ripple gait is used consistently throughout the subsequent documentation.

In ripple gait, the six legs of the hexapod robot swing in a coordinated sequence, generating a wave-like trajectory. This gait enables stable locomotion across varied terrain and environments while improving walking efficiency and smoothness.

During ripple gait locomotion, each leg swings according to a specific rhythm and sequence. In each step, forward legs advance while rear legs push backward, producing undulating dynamics. This gait helps the robot maintain equilibrium and traverse undulating ground.

Key advantages of the ripple gait for hexapod robots include:

  1. High stability: Because multiple legs contact the ground simultaneously, the robot resists tipping over and maintains balance on uneven surfaces.

  2. High efficiency: The ripple gait adapts effectively to terrain contours, reducing energy expenditure. The wave-like foot trajectory facilitates overcoming obstacles and climbing slopes.

  3. Exceptional agility: The ripple gait enables smooth changes in heading and velocity, enhancing robot responsiveness during obstacle avoidance and target tracking in confined spaces.

When JetHexa operates in ripple gait, each pair of opposing legs acts alternately. The ripple gait sequence is analyzed below:

As shown in Phase A, legs 2 and 4 lift and swing forward, while legs 1, 3, 5, and 6 support the chassis, ensuring that the center of gravity of the robot stays within the diagonal intersection. At this moment, legs 2 and 4 are in the swing phase, and legs 1, 3, 5, and 6 are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase B, legs 3 and 6 lift and swing forward, while legs 1, 2, 4, and 5 support the chassis, ensuring that the center of gravity remains centered. At this moment, legs 3 and 6 are in the swing phase, and legs 1, 2, 4, and 5 are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase C, legs 1 and 5 lift and swing forward, while legs 2, 3, 4, and 6 support the chassis, maintaining balance. At this moment, legs 1 and 5 are in the swing phase, and legs 2, 3, 4, and 6 are in the stance phase.

As shown in Phase D, legs 2 and 6 lift and swing forward, while legs 1, 3, 4, and 5 support the chassis, maintaining stability. At this moment, legs 2 and 6 are in the swing phase, and legs 1, 3, 4, and 5 are in the stance phase.

2.18 Static Posture Parameter Description ​

2.18.1 Program Overview ​

The posture parameters of JetHexa refer to state parameters when the robot remains stationary, including standing height, pitch angle, and roll angle.

Adjusting the foothold coordinate values of the six legs of JetHexa alters the overall posture of the robot. Posture configuration requires invoking the step_controller.py file, which is located at: /home/ubuntu/ros2_ws/src/driver/controller/controller/step_controller.py

python
def transform_pose_euler(self, translate, axis, euler, duration, degrees=True):
        """
        Changes the robot posture using translation transformation and Euler angles.
        :param translate: Translation transformation of body center offset (x, y, z)
        :param axis: Sequence of three Euler angle axes, such as 'xyz' or 'yzx'
        :param euler: Tuple of Euler angles, where the order must match axis
        :param duration: Duration required to complete this transformation
        :param degrees: Specifies whether the Euler angle unit is degrees. True for degrees, False for radians
        """
        rotate = R.from_euler(axis, euler, degrees=degrees)  # Uses Euler angles to create a transformer
        r = rotate.as_euler('xyz', degrees=False)  # Converts into fixed-order Euler angles
        generator = PoseTransformer(PoseTransformerParams(translation=translate, rotation=r, duration=duration))

        if generator:
            with self.lock:
                generator.send(None)

def launch_setup(context):
    compiled = os.environ['need_compile']
    debug = LaunchConfiguration('debug', default='false')
    debug_arg = DeclareLaunchArgument('debug', default_value=debug)

    if compiled == 'True':
        controller_package_path = get_package_share_directory('controller')
    else:
        controller_package_path = '/home/ubuntu/ros2_ws/src/driver/controller'

    self_balancing_node = GroupAction([

        IncludeLaunchDescription(
            PythonLaunchDescriptionSource(
                os.path.join(controller_package_path, 'launch/controller.launch.py')),
            condition=IfCondition(debug),
            ),

        Node(
            package='app',
            executable='self_balancing',
            output='screen',
            parameters=[{'debug': debug}],
            ),
    ])

    return [debug_arg,
            self_balancing_node,
            ]

  1. Parameters of the transform_euler function:
  • pose: Specifies the target posture to transform.
  • translate: Specifies the translation transformation of the body center offset, measured in millimeters, with the allowable range determined by the current robot physical posture. The valid range for X and Y is -50 to 50 mm, and the valid range for Z is -30 to 90 mm.
  • axis: Specifies the sequence of the Euler angle axes.
  • euler: Specifies a tuple of Euler angles representing the rotation angle around each axis. The sequence and length must match the axis parameter. Positive values indicate counterclockwise rotation and negative values indicate clockwise rotation. The valid range is -20° to 20°.
  • degrees: Specifies whether the Euler angle unit is degrees. Setting True uses degrees, while False uses radians.
