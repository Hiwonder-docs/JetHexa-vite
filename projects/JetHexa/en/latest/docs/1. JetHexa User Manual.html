1.1 Product Overview ​

1.1.1 Overview of JetHexa ​

JetHexa Starter Kit is a comprehensive hexapod robot developed for ROS educational scenarios. It runs Ubuntu 22.04 LTS and comes pre-installed with the ROS 2 Humble robotics operating system.

Powered by the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano/Orin NX controller, high-voltage bus servos, and a monocular camera, it enables robotic motion control, human body feature recognition, and body posture interaction. Integrated with OpenCV and YOLO26, it performs color recognition, object tracking, and waste sorting, fulfilling requirements for learning and validating ROS 2 and machine vision algorithms.

For locomotion, it employs a hexapod ripple gait with adjustable body posture, height, and speed, delivering an enhanced control experience.

JetHexa Standard Kit is a comprehensive hexapod robot developed for ROS educational scenarios. It runs Ubuntu 22.04 LTS and comes pre-installed with the ROS 2 Humble robotics operating system.

Powered by high-performance hardware configurations including the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano/Orin NX controller, high-voltage bus servos, a COIN-D6 LiDAR, a monocular camera, and a WonderEcho Pro AI voice interaction box, it enables robotic motion control, mapping and navigation, tracking and obstacle avoidance, autonomous cruising, human body feature recognition, body posture interaction, voice control, and large AI model applications. Integrated with OpenCV and YOLO26, it performs color recognition, object tracking, and waste sorting, fulfilling requirements for learning and validating ROS 2 and machine vision algorithms.

For locomotion, it employs a hexapod ripple gait with adjustable body posture, height, and speed, delivering an enhanced control experience.

1.3 Basic Operation Guide ​

This section covers the startup status of the robot, hardware module verification, app remote control, and wireless controller operation.

To connect to the robot using remote tools, explore advanced features, or inspect program source code, refer to Section 1.4 Development Environment Setup.

1.3.1 Charging Instructions ​

The battery is disconnected and partially discharged prior to shipping to ensure safety during transit. Before initial operation, connect the battery power cables and fully charge the battery. Charging the battery from 10 V to approximately 12.3 V takes 2–3 hours.

1.3.1.1 Lithium Battery Safety Guidelines ​

NOTE

  • Use only the dedicated charger included in the kit and ensure that the robot is powered off during charging. Never operate the robot while charging. The charger indicator glows red during charging and turns green once charging is complete.

  • Disconnect the charger promptly after completion. If the battery voltage drops below 10 V or the buzzer beeps continuously, stop operation immediately and recharge the battery.

  • For long-term storage, charge the battery fully, disconnect all cables, and store it in a cool, dry environment free of strong static electricity or magnetic fields, away from high temperatures and liquids.

  • Never impact, drop, step on, disassemble, modify, or solder the battery or charger. Never connect the battery directly to an AC power outlet or short-circuit the positive and negative terminals with metal objects to prevent severe damage, overheating, or fire.

Solemn Statement: Failure to adhere to the aforementioned safety guidelines and operating standards may result in equipment damage, financial loss, or safety accidents, for which the manufacturer assumes no liability.

1.3.1.2 Charging Procedure ​

NOTE

Before powering on the robot for the first time, ensure that the battery cables located at the bottom of the chassis are firmly connected. Remove the two bottom screws to open the sheet metal compartment and inspect the connections.

  1. Ensure that the power switch on the robot is toggled to OFF.
  1. Insert the charging plug of the 12.6 V charger into the charging port on the side of the STM32 controller.
  1. Observe the LED indicator on the charger to monitor the charging progress. A red light indicates charging in progress, and a green light indicates that charging is complete.

NOTE

Disconnect the charger promptly once charging is complete to prevent overcharging.

1.3.2 Initial Power-On and Inspection ​

1.3.2.1 Pre-Power-On Precautions ​

Before powering on, inspect all robotic joints to confirm proper installation and clear all obstacles within the operating workspace. Never forcefully twist or pull the servo joints manually after power-on. Avoid grasping excessive loads, operating at limit angles for extended periods, or running the servos under mechanical stall conditions. If stuttering, continuous jitter, abnormal noise, positional deviation, significant servo overheating, smoke, or unusual odors occur during operation, terminate the program immediately and turn off the power. Contact technical support if necessary to avoid servo damage caused by prolonged stall or high-load operation.

1.3.3.2 Connection Modes Overview ​

The robot supports two networking modes:

  1. AP direct mode: The development board broadcasts a Wi-Fi hotspot for direct smartphone connection, without external Internet access.
  2. STA LAN mode: The development board connects to a designated local Wi-Fi router or hotspot, enabling external Internet access.

The robot defaults to AP direct mode. All robot features and interactive modes operate identically under both AP and STA modes. Connecting via AP direct mode first is recommended for initial feature testing. STA LAN mode can be configured subsequently as needed.

The following procedure demonstrates connection using an Android device, and the same steps apply to iOS devices.

1.3.5 Wireless Controller Operation ​

1.3.5.1 Precautions ​

  1. Confirm that the USB wireless controller receiver is inserted into the USB port prior to powering on the robot. The receiver is pre-installed at the factory.
  2. Observe proper battery polarity when inserting AAA batteries into the controller.
  1. The system automatically launches the background app service on startup, which includes the wireless controller service. Unless manually terminated, the controller service connects automatically.
  2. Because wireless controllers may experience cross-talk, avoid using this feature when multiple robots operate in the same area to prevent accidental connection and unintended control.
  3. If the controller is not paired within 30 s after powering on, or remains idle for more than 5 min after pairing, it enters sleep mode automatically. Press the START button to wake the controller.

1.3.5.2 Device Pairing ​

  1. After the robot completes its boot sequence, toggle the power switch on the wireless controller to ON. Both the red and green LED indicators flash simultaneously.
  2. After several seconds, the robot and controller complete automatic pairing. The green LED remains solid, and the red LED turns off upon successful pairing.

1.5 Quick Mapping and Navigation ​

1.5.1 Quick Mapping Experience ​

This section provides a quick walkthrough of 2D LiDAR SLAM mapping and autonomous navigation without requiring complex terminal operations, as features can be launched directly from desktop shortcut icons.

During quick mapping, control robot locomotion using the wireless controller or the keyboard. For single-robot operation, the wireless controller is recommended. In multi-robot environments, keyboard control prevents wireless signal interference across units.

Once mapping finishes, save the map file to disk. The autonomous navigation package loads the most recently saved map by default. Creating and saving a new map overwrites previous map files.

Manual mapping utilizes the slam_toolbox package. The slam_toolbox package subscribes to LaserScan messages from the LiDAR and applies coordinate transformations from odom to base_link to generate a 2D occupancy grid map of the environment. The package supports full serialization of pose graphs and map data for continuous mapping, relocalization, and multi-session map merging. It operates in synchronous mode, processing all valid sensor measurements regardless of processing lag, and asynchronous mode, processing sensor measurements as computational bandwidth permits.

For algorithmic principles, launch commands, and multiple map management, refer to 5. Mapping and Navigation Course.

1.7.2 Electronic Control System Overview ​

The electronic control architecture adopts a dual-brain design: the Jetson Orin Nano/Orin NX controller acts as the host controller managing LiDAR, the 3D depth camera, and voice peripherals, while the STM32 controller acts as the sub-controller driving the multi-channel bus servos and integrating a 6-axis IMU for real-time motion control, attitude feedback, and sensor acquisition.

1.7.2.1 ROS Controller ​

Jetson Orin Nano/Orin NX:

The JetHexa robot employs the Jetson Orin Nano/Orin NX controller paired with a dedicated expansion board. The compute module runs modern deep learning frameworks to deliver ample edge AI compute power for machine vision and large AI model tasks.

The expansion board breaks out status LEDs and tactile buttons, enabling network status monitoring and connection mode switching, while providing exposed GPIO, I2C, and LiDAR serial communication headers.

The controller runs Ubuntu 22.04.5 LTS with ROS 2 Humble pre-installed.

For controller fundamentals, refer to Development Basics Course.

1.7.2.2 STM32 Robot Controller ​

The STM32 controller acts as the motion sub-controller. It features an on-board STM32F407VET6 MCU, PWM and bus servo driver circuits, and a QMI8658 6-axis IMU motion sensor.

The sub-controller receives high-level velocity and trajectory commands from the ROS controller or wireless controller via UART to drive the 18 chassis bus servos and 2 pan-tilt servos, while supplying power to the host controller. Multi-function programmable buttons are also available for custom programmable functions.

1.7.2.3 Aurora930 Pro 3D Depth Camera ​

Vision serves as the primary perceptual sensor of the robot.

The robot utilizes an Aurora930 Pro 3D depth camera based on 3D structured light technology. By fusing RGB color images with calibrated depth maps, it provides dense 3D spatial perception for obstacle avoidance, visual SLAM, and spatial tracking.

In software, it supports OpenCV vision tasks, deep learning object detection, and KCF target tracking. For camera tutorials, refer to 4. Camera Basics Course.

1.7.2.4 LiDAR Sensors ​

The robot supports two LiDAR options: the COIN-D6 LiDAR, also designated as LD19, and the RPLIDAR C1. The LiDAR sensor emits modulated laser pulses to measure distance and bearing, generating 360° 2D point cloud scans. Applications include obstacle avoidance, target following, 2D SLAM mapping, and autonomous navigation. For details, refer to 3. LiDAR Course.

The LD19 LiDAR incorporates a ranging core, wireless power transfer coil, wireless optical transceiver, angle measurement unit, motor driver, and protective casing. After power-on, the scan frequency stabilizes to 10 ± 0.1 Hz within 3 s. It provides an external PWM interface for closed-loop speed regulation using a PID controller.

The LD19 interfaces via a ZH1.5T-4P 1.5 mm connector for DC power and serial data:

The RPLIDAR C1 is a hybrid 2D LiDAR featuring a high-speed laser ranging sampling capability of 5,000 samples per second. It adopts non-contact energy and signal transmission technology, overcoming the lifespan limitations of traditional LiDAR to achieve reliable, stable long-term operation.

The RPLIDAR C1 provides a ranging radius of up to 12 m with a minimum blind zone down to 0.05 m, easily scanning and ranging objects across various distances to address diverse obstacle challenges in the environment. While maintaining powerful performance, the RPLIDAR C1 features a compact and agile form factor, ultra-low noise, and minimal vibration, making it easy to embed into various applications. It can be used in practical applications such as robot vacuum cleaners, educational ROS wheeled robots, commercial robots, low-speed park autonomous vehicles, and parking space detection.
